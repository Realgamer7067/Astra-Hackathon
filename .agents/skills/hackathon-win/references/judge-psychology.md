# Judge Psychology and Attack Mode

Use to sharpen the story, simulate questions, and expose weak proof.

## Cognitive simplicity

Judges may see many projects and have limited attention. Make the product easy to encode and recall:

- one specific user;
- one painful moment;
- one-sentence product;
- one clear before/after;
- one real technical contribution;
- one wow moment.

Write the memory hook in `PITCH.md` and repeat it naturally at reveal and close. A judge should be able to explain the project to another judge afterward.

## Skeptical-judge questions

Generate and answer at least:

- Who needs this, and how do you know the problem is real?
- Why does this solution create visible value?
- What already exists, and what is meaningfully different?
- Why is AI or this technical approach necessary?
- What did the team build during the event?
- Does the central feature actually work? Show the proof.
- What is real, simulated, mocked, cached, or prerecorded?
- Where does data come from, and what are its limits?
- How do you handle incorrect model output or failure?
- Why this sponsor technology?
- What about privacy, safety, and security?
- How would this scale, and what is deliberately not production-ready?
- What is the business/adoption model if relevant?
- What would you build next?

Challenge vague answers. Replace adjectives with evidence and tradeoffs. If the answer exposes a demo-visible weakness, prioritize it by rubric weight and time mode.

## Judge lenses

Research publicly available professional roles of named judges only when useful. Use them to prepare domain vocabulary and likely questions, not to manipulate, profile, or assume personal preferences. The official rubric remains authoritative.

## Attack sequence

1. Give the explanation 15 seconds. If unclear, rewrite the memory hook.
2. Ask why the product is not an obvious wrapper or copy.
3. Trace the central claim through the architecture and code evidence.
4. Trigger the top dependency failure and run the fallback.
5. Ask what was built during the event and verify commit history.
6. Score the presentation against `RUBRIC.md` without giving credit for invisible work.
7. Identify the single question most likely to damage credibility and repair the answer or product.

Do not coach deceptive answers. Honest, precise limitations usually increase credibility.
