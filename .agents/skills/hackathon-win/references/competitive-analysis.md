# Competitive Analysis

Use during idea selection and again when shaping differentiation and judge answers.

## Search surface

Look for:

- previous projects from this event and adjacent hackathons;
- current commercial products and startup competitors;
- relevant GitHub repositories and templates;
- academic or public-sector systems where applicable;
- common solution patterns for the problem statement.

Search the exact proposed product sentence, the user problem, the core technical method, and combinations of sponsor/API + theme. Prefer primary product pages, repositories, papers, and official winner showcases.

## `COMPETITION.md`

For each meaningful comparator capture:

| Comparator | User / claim | What it already does | Common or boring pattern | Our meaningful difference | Evidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Then answer:

- What already exists?
- What will many teams probably build?
- Which approach has become visually or narratively generic?
- What do we do differently that changes user value, not just branding?
- Can that difference be demonstrated in under one minute?
- Which sentence should the pitch use to explain it?

## Differentiation rules

Good differentiation is observable: a new workflow, stronger result, unique data, real action, better verification, lower latency, offline/edge operation, collaboration, accessibility, or a striking interaction.

Weak differentiation is adjective-based: "smarter," "seamless," "AI-powered," or "all-in-one" without proof.

Do not attack competitors unfairly or claim they lack features without verification. Position the project against a category and a user gap, then show evidence.

## Time limit

Stop once the team can name the dominant alternatives, the boring/common pattern, and one defensible demo-visible difference. More market mapping is production-only unless business viability is heavily weighted.
